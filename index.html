Exhaust, or signal?

CO2 is commonly thought of as a toxin.

Very few biologists recognize carbon dioxide’s role as a fundamental, universal protective factor. It may be helpful to consider some of the ways carbon dioxide might be controlling situations that otherwise are poorly understood.

Increasing carbon dioxide doesn’t necessarily cause acidosis, and acidosis caused by carbon dioxide isn’t as harmful as lactic acidosis.

When CO2 runs low

Scroll: the cell beside the text slides from rich to low CO2, and the branches draw themselves.

richlow

  1. Falling CO2 raises lactic acid, which promotes inflammation and fibrosis; a prolonged fall makes proteins more prone to glycation, and probably adds to the histone methylation that increases with age.

    In eccentric exercise and denervation, less oxygen is used and less carbon dioxide is produced, while lactic acid increases, displacing carbon dioxide, and more fat is oxidized. Prolonged stress similarly decreases carbon dioxide and increases lactate, while increasing the use of fat.

  2. Branch: lactic acid

    On a slightly longer time scale, a reduction of carbon dioxide can increase the production of lactic acid, which is a promoter of inflammation and fibrosis.

  3. Branch: a prolonged fall

    A prolonged decrease in carbon dioxide can increase the susceptibility of proteins to glycation (the addition of aldehydes, from polyunsaturated fat peroxidation or methylglyoxal from lactate metabolism, to amino groups), and a similar process is likely to contribute to the methylation of histones, a process that increases with aging.

    The histone link at the end is speculative.

  4. Side branch: methylation

    With aging, DNA methylation is increased.

    One possibility is that methylation stabilizes and protects cells when growth and regeneration aren’t possible, and that it’s likely to increase when CO2 isn’t available.

  5. About the drawing

    The methyl “locks” that settle onto the DNA in the cell drawing are our illustration, not a picture of real chemistry.

    On this view, methylation is not a malfunction: it may be protective when growth isn’t possible, and aging is tied to genes being progressively silenced (see the womb section below).

    There are no studies of carbon dioxide levels in people who practise tai chi; the idea that it involves hypoventilation is an inference, not a finding.

Night

Drag the hand around the clock, or focus it and use the arrow keys.

Daytime. Move the hand into the shaded night arc.

Darkness is stressful and catabolic.

In restful sleep, the oxygen tension is frequently low enough, and the carbon dioxide tension high enough, to trigger the multiplication of stem cells and mitochondria.

During the rapid-eye-movement (dreaming) phase of sleep, breathing is inhibited, and the level of carbon dioxide in the tissues accumulates.

The active, dreaming phase of sleep occurs more frequently in the later part of the night, as morning approaches.

Animals

    Closed burrows, rich in carbon dioxide.

    Naked mole rats

    They live in burrows that are kept closed, so the percentage of oxygen is lower than in the outside air, and the percentage of carbon dioxide ranges from 0.2% to 5% (atmospheric CO2 is about 0.038).

    In captivity they have lived up to 30 years, 9 or 10 times longer than mice of the same size; a mole rat has never been known to develop cancer.

    Lab measurements may not reflect their natural metabolism, and their high CO2 may compensate for living in the dark.

  • The hive

    Workers regulate the hive’s carbon dioxide.

    CO2 → queen’s ovaries (not her lifespan)

    The hive

    In beehives, the workers carefully regulate the carbon dioxide concentration, which varies from about 0.2% to 6%, similar to that of the mole rat colony.

    Hive CO2 is linked to activating the queen’s ovaries; queen longevity is tied to the type of fat in her tissues, not to CO2.

    Bats

    Bats have a very high metabolic rate, and an extremely long lifespan for an animal of their size.

    Their high serum CO2 is tied to protection from cave ammonia, not to their lifespan.

  • Birds

    Faster metabolism than mammals their size, and longer lives.

    Long life → fats, not CO2

    Turn over ↻

    Birds

    Birds have a higher metabolic rate than mammals of the same size, and live longer.

    Birds’ long lives come up in the context of fewer highly unsaturated fats, not CO2.

The womb

The normal intrauterine level of carbon dioxide is high, and it can be increased or decreased by changes in the mother’s physiology.

There is some evidence of increased demethylation associated with the low level of oxygen in the uterus.

If the intrauterine experience, with low oxygen and high carbon dioxide, serves to “reprogram” cells to remove the accumulated effects of age and stress, and so to maximize the developmental potential of the new organism, a life that’s lived with nearly those levels of oxygen and carbon dioxide might be able to avoid the progressive silencing of genes and loss of function that cause aging and degenerative diseases.
